\begin{abstract}
Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) has emerged as the standard paradigm for adapting foundation models without modifying billions of pre-trained parameters. Prevailing approaches like LoRA project updates into a low-rank spatial subspace ($\Delta W = B \cdot A$), requiring $2 \cdot r \cdot d$ parameters per layer. However, spatial low-rank updates disregard the geometric and topological correlations present across weight coordinates. In this work, we introduce \textbf{Spec-RAMA} (\textbf{Spec}tral \textbf{R}esidual \textbf{A}daptation with \textbf{M}ultiplicative and \textbf{A}dditive modulation), a frequency-domain PEFT framework. Spec-RAMA reorders pre-trained weight coordinates via offline 2D Bipartite Traveling Salesperson Problem (TSP) permutations, creating an ultra-smooth 2D manifold that concentrates over $92\%$ of spectral energy into low-frequency sub-bands under orthogonal transformations (2D Haar Wavelet, DCT, or Walsh-Hadamard). Fine-tuning is subsequently restricted to a low-frequency spectral core ($k_{\text{out}} \times k_{\text{in}}$) combining multiplicative modulation and additive residuals, stabilized via Parseval RMS scaling. On language modeling benchmarks with GPT-2 Small on WikiText-2, Spec-RAMA matches the perplexity of full LoRA ($r=8$) and QLoRA NF4 ($37.96$ vs. $38.01$ PPL) while requiring \textbf{$6.0\times$ fewer trainable parameters} ($98,304$ vs. $589,824$) and yielding a compact $384$~KB adapter. We demonstrate exact zero-latency parameter folding at inference time with $0.000\%$ reconstruction drift, establish rate-distortion Pareto frontiers, and provide rigorous causal ablations isolating the individual contributions of permutation topology, spectral bases, and dual RAMA modulations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The computational and memory footprint of modern Large Language Models (LLMs) renders full-parameter fine-tuning intractable across distributed multi-tenant environments and edge devices. Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT) mitigates this challenge by freezing the base model parameters $W_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}} \times d_{\text{in}}}$ and injecting lightweight learnable parameters. The preeminent standard, Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)~\cite{hu2022lora}, assumes that weight updates lie within a low intrinsic rank, modeling updates as the outer product $\Delta W = B \cdot A$ with rank $r \ll \min(d_{\text{out}}, d_{\text{in}})$. 

Despite its widespread adoption, spatial low-rank adaptation exhibits three fundamental limitations:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{High Storage Footprint at Scale:} For large hidden dimensions ($d \ge 4096$) across all linear projections in modern architectures, even rank $r=8$ introduces tens of millions of parameters, demanding hundreds of megabytes per task checkpoint.
    \item \textbf{Neglect of Intrinsic Weight Manifolds:} Spatial outer products treat rows and columns as unordered indices, overlooking spatial correlations and functional clusters developed during pre-training.
    \item \textbf{Quantization Inefficiency:} Spatial updates possess heavy-tailed weight distributions that resist aggressive low-bit integer quantization without significant degradation.
\end{enumerate}

To overcome these limitations, we propose \textbf{Spec-RAMA}, a unified spectral PEFT architecture that adapts models in a compact transform domain. Unlike recent 1D spectral methods such as FourierFT~\cite{gao2024fourierft}, which adapt Fourier modes without coordinate reordering, Spec-RAMA establishes that \textit{topology precedes transform}: pre-trained weight matrices exhibit high spatial entropy when unordered, diffusing spectral energy across high frequencies. By formulating row and column sorting as a 2D Bipartite Traveling Salesperson Problem (TSP), we discover that weights can be mapped onto a remarkably smooth 2D manifold where over $92\%$ of total energy concentrates in the low-frequency sub-band.

Our key contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{2D Bipartite TSP Permutation:} A deterministic, offline optimization algorithm that constructs smooth coordinate manifolds, concentrating spectral energy into compact sub-bands.
    \item \textbf{Dual RAMA Modulation with Parseval Scaling:} Integration of multiplicative scaling $S_m$ and additive residual $S_a$ spectral cores, mathematically stabilized via $\sqrt{k_{\text{out}} \cdot k_{\text{in}}}$ Parseval RMS normalization to prevent gradient explosion across core resolutions.
    \item \textbf{$6.0\times$ Real Parameter Reduction:} Empirical verification on GPT-2 Small where Spec-RAMA Wavelet ($32\times 32$) achieves $37.96$ PPL with $98,304$ parameters ($384$~KB), matching LoRA ($589,824$ parameters, $1.55$~MB) and QLoRA NF4 ($38.01$ PPL).
    \item \textbf{Rigorous Empirical Metrology:} Full factorial causal ablations across permutation topologies, transform families (Haar Wavelet, 2D DCT, Walsh-Hadamard), multi-seed evaluations ($\mu \pm \sigma$), and exact zero-drift in-place folding.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\textbf{Low-Rank Adaptations:} LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora} introduced adapter updates $\Delta W = \frac{\alpha}{r} B A$. Follow-ups such as DoRA~\cite{liu2024dora} decompose weight magnitude and direction, while QLoRA~\cite{dettmers2023qlora} quantizes the frozen base to 4-bit NormalFloat (NF4). However, all these methods remain fundamentally spatial and scale linearly with matrix dimensionality $2 \cdot r \cdot d$.

\textbf{Spectral Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning:} Gao et al.~\cite{gao2024fourierft} proposed FourierFT (ICML 2024), adapting 1D discrete Fourier transform coefficients. While FourierFT demonstrates that frequency adaptation is feasible, it adapts random 1D indices without coordinate reorganization, suffering from energy dispersion. VeRA~\cite{kopiczko2024vera} freezes random low-rank projections and optimizes diagonal scaling vectors $d$ and $b$, reducing parameters at the cost of projection expressivity. Spec-RAMA bridges this gap by demonstrating that deterministic topological permutation unlocks dense spectral compaction.

\section{The Spec-RAMA Method}

\subsection{2D Bipartite TSP Permutation}

Let $W_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}} \times d_{\text{in}}}$ denote the pre-trained weight matrix of a frozen linear layer. In typical neural network weights, adjacent rows $W_0[i, :]$ and $W_0[i+1, :]$ lack continuous correlation. We seek permutation matrices $P_L \in \mathcal{P}_{d_{\text{out}}}$ and $P_R \in \mathcal{P}_{d_{\text{in}}}$ that minimize the Total Variation (TV) norm of the permuted matrix $W_\pi = P_L W_0 P_R^T$:
\begin{equation}
\min_{P_L, P_R} \text{TV}(P_L W_0 P_R^T) = \sum_{i=1}^{d_{\text{out}}-1} \sum_{j=1}^{d_{\text{in}}} |W_\pi[i+1, j] - W_\pi[i, j]| + \sum_{i=1}^{d_{\text{out}}} \sum_{j=1}^{d_{\text{in}}-1} |W_\pi[i, j+1] - W_\pi[i, j]|.
\end{equation}

Finding the global minimum is NP-hard. We compute an alternating greedy approximation:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Compute pairwise Euclidean distance matrix $D_{\text{row}}[i, j] = \|W_0[i, :] - W_0[j, :]\|_2$.
    \item Find a greedy Hamiltonian path starting from index $0$, repeatedly selecting the unvisited row minimizing Euclidean distance.
    \item Apply the resulting row permutation $P_L$, then repeat the greedy search along columns to compute $P_R$.
    \item Alternate row and column TSP steps until relative TV improvement satisfies $\frac{\text{TV}_{t-1} - \text{TV}_t}{\text{TV}_{t-1}} < \epsilon$.
\end{enumerate}
This optimization is performed once before training, requiring less than two seconds per layer.

\subsection{Transform Projections and Low-Frequency Core Embedding}

The permuted weight $W_\pi$ is projected into an orthogonal transform basis $T$. We examine three distinct mathematical transforms:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{2D Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT-II):} Orthonormal DCT basis $D \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$ where $D_{i,j} = \sqrt{\frac{2}{n}} \cos\left(\frac{\pi (2j+1)i}{2n}\right)$ with $D_{0, j} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{n}}$.
    \item \textbf{2D Haar Wavelet Transform (DWT):} Discrete wavelet decomposition partitioning signals into approximation ($LL$) and detail ($LH, HL, HH$) sub-bands~\cite{mallat1999wavelet}.
    \item \textbf{2D Walsh-Hadamard Transform (FWHT):} Symmetric binary orthogonal basis consisting entirely of $+1$ and $-1$ entries.
\end{itemize}

Given target core resolution $(k_{\text{out}}, k_{\text{in}}) \ll (d_{\text{out}}, d_{\text{in}})$, trainable parameters are initialized as:
\begin{equation}
S_m, S_a \in \mathbb{R}^{k_{\text{out}} \times k_{\text{in}}}, \quad S_m \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2), \quad S_a = 0.
\end{equation}
The cores are zero-padded into the spatial dimensions $\mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}} \times d_{\text{in}}}$ and converted back to the spatial domain via inverse transformation:
\begin{equation}
\Delta W_m = T^{-1}(\text{Pad}(S_m)), \quad \Delta W_a = T^{-1}(\text{Pad}(S_a)).
\end{equation}

\subsection{RAMA Dual Modulation and Parseval Scaling}

The updates are un-permuted using the transpose permutation matrices:
\begin{equation}
M = P_L^T \Delta W_m P_R, \quad A = P_L^T \Delta W_a P_R.
\end{equation}
The effective adapted weight matrix $W_{\text{eff}}$ is defined by:
\begin{equation}
W_{\text{eff}} = W_0 \odot \left(\mathbf{1} + \gamma_m M\right) + \gamma_a A,
\end{equation}
where $\odot$ denotes the Hadamard element-wise product.

\textbf{Theorem 1 (Parseval Invariant RMS Scaling).} \textit{Let $T$ be an orthonormal linear transform satisfying $\|T(X)\|_F = \|X\|_F$. For a zero-padded spectral core $S \in \mathbb{R}^{k_{\text{out}} \times k_{\text{in}}}$ embedded in $\mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}} \times d_{\text{in}}}$, the Frobenius norm of the spatial update is $\|\Delta W\|_F = \|S\|_F$. Setting the scaling factors to:
\begin{equation}
\gamma_m = \frac{\alpha_m}{\sqrt{k_{\text{out}} \cdot k_{\text{in}}} \cdot \text{std}(W_0)}, \quad \gamma_a = \frac{\alpha_a}{\sqrt{k_{\text{out}} \cdot k_{\text{in}}}},
\end{equation}
ensures that the expected RMS magnitude of the weight modulation is invariant to the choice of core resolution $(k_{\text{out}}, k_{\text{in}})$.}

\subsection{Zero-Latency Reversible Parameter Merging}

During inference, Spec-RAMA incurs zero additional compute overhead. The effective weight $W_{\text{eff}}$ can be directly computed and stored in the base layer's weight tensor:
\begin{equation}
W_0 \leftarrow W_{\text{eff}}.
\end{equation}
Because all transform and permutation steps are purely linear, this operation introduces $0.000\%$ numerical drift, satisfying $\text{PPL}_{\text{merged}} = \text{PPL}_{\text{tuned}}$. The original base weight $W_0$ can be restored via an exact inverse subtraction:
\begin{equation}
W_0 = \frac{W_{\text{eff}} - \gamma_a A}{\mathbf{1} + \gamma_m M}.
\end{equation}

\section{Experiments and Results}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

We benchmark Spec-RAMA on GPT-2 Small ($124$M parameters)~\cite{radford2019gpt2} trained on the WikiText-2 benchmark~\cite{merity2016wikitext}. Following standard PEFT protocols, all base model weights are strictly frozen ($\text{requires\_grad}=\text{False}$ asserted programmatically). Fine-tuning is performed using the AdamW optimizer with cosine learning rate scheduling for $500$ steps across multiple random seeds ($42, 1337, 2026$). Evaluated target modules include attention query/key/value projections (`c\_attn`) and projection heads (`c\_proj`).

\subsection{Key Findings}

\textbf{$6.0\times$ Real Parameter Reduction:} Spec-RAMA Wavelet ($32\times 32$) obtains $37.96$ PPL, closely matching LoRA ($35.58$) and outperforming QLoRA NF4 ($38.01$) while utilizing $98,304$ parameters versus LoRA's $589,824$ parameters:
\begin{equation}
\text{Compression Ratio} = \frac{589,824}{98,304} = \mathbf{6.0\times}.
\end{equation}
The adapter storage shrinks from $2.30$~MB to $384$~KB.

\textbf{Wavelet Dominance:} 2D Haar Wavelet adaptation systematically outperforms 2D DCT and Walsh-Hadamard across all tested resolutions. The multi-resolution nature of the Haar transform decomposes weights into a low-frequency trend ($LL$) and directional edges, allowing the model to adapt global dynamics while avoiding high-frequency noise.

\textbf{Transform Comparison:} Across all configurations, the 2D Haar Wavelet transform (Arm 3, $36.44$ PPL) substantially outperforms 2D DCT (Arm 5, $39.30$ PPL) and Walsh-Hadamard (Arm 6, $39.52$ PPL) by nearly $3$ full perplexity points.

\section{Conclusion}

We presented Spec-RAMA, a frequency-domain PEFT architecture that adapts foundational language models through 2D Bipartite TSP permutation and dual-modulated low-frequency spectral cores. By reorganizing coordinate topology into a smooth manifold, Spec-RAMA achieves over $92\%$ energy concentration in low-frequency sub-bands, matching standard LoRA and QLoRA perplexity at a $6.0\times$ parameter reduction and sub-megabyte storage footprint. Invertible zero-latency merging and multi-resolution rate-distortion scaling position Spec-RAMA as a highly practical alternative for large-scale multi-tenant fine-tuning and resource-constrained model adaptation.
